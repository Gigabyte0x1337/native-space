# Symbolic Prime-Pattern Sign Route

## One law, not a prime range

Native Space contains the one generative object D-PRIME-PATTERN-1. A positive
input $k$ observes that pattern. The proof below keeps $k$ symbolic and
proves one finite formula; it does not construct, traverse, or aggregate a
native range of primes.

`INDEX(k)` selects identity and multiplicative direction. Reapplying that
INDEX changes depth. It does not mean “advance to another prime.” The
prime-value camera $p(k)$ and wrapped orientation $\mathbf J^k$ are
separate observations of the same symbolic input.

### D-NATIVE-RE-1 -- native reflected-equality pattern [Definition]

Let $\kappa(s)=\sigma+it$. At symbolic observation $k$, the two reflected
prime-log coordinates from D-DUAL-CHAR-1 are

$$
p(k)^{-\sigma}e^{-it\log p(k)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
p(k)^{-(1-\sigma)}e^{-it\log p(k)}.
$$

Their mismatch has the common nonzero analytic phase
$e^{-it\log p(k)}$. Native inverse MULTIPLY removes that phase and leaves
the real gain law

$$
g(\sigma;k):=p(k)^{-\sigma}-p(k)^{-(1-\sigma)}.
$$

Define the deterministic dephased pattern by

$$
\mathcal P_s(k):=g(\sigma;k).
$$

The notation $\mathcal P_s\equiv\mathbf0$ means that this symbolic law is the
zero law on its observation domain. It does not denote an aggregate or an
infinite stored vector. The sign theorem below is independent of the selected
positive input, so one observation is sufficient to test its zero locus.

The source examples `prime_pattern`, `dual_prime_alignment`, and
`remove_prime_log_phase` expand to INDEX, ORIENT, MULTIPLY, and ADD. They do not
claim the open zeta-to-RE implication. The wrapped
orientation $i^k$ is not the phase removed here: it is pattern metadata,
whereas $e^{-it\log p(k)}$ is generated by the analytic character.

### Finite four-orientation cancellation [Proved consequence]

For a finite coefficient input, the four wrapped orientations obey one
periodic rule:

$$
\mathbf1,\ \mathbf J,\ \mathbf{-1},\ \mathbf{-J},\ \mathbf1,\ldots
$$

The oriented ADD is zero exactly when the `1/-1` lane balances and the
`J/-J` lane balances. This is the finite consequence of
T-ORIENT-ZERO-1 and T-AXIS-ADD-CANCEL-1; it does not reuse or redefine
T-AXIS-CANCEL-1, which remains the separate two-dimensional projection theorem
in `13-native-rh-geometry.md`. In particular T-ORIENT-ZERO-1 proves

$$
\mathbf1\boxplus\mathbf J\boxplus\mathbf{-1}\boxplus\mathbf{-J}
=\mathbf0.
$$

For unequal gains the orientation cycle alone is insufficient: opposite
orientations cancel only when their gains agree. T-OPPOSITE-PAIR-1 proves this
condition, and T-ALL-PERSPECTIVES-1 proves that changing a common observer
only rotates the same zero equation.

This also explains why independent dephasing is not generically
zero-preserving after a scalar projection:

$$
1+i-1-i=0,
\qquad
1+1+1+1=4.
$$

The finite native counterexample and its exact scope are proved in
`19-zeta-zero-fiber-audit.md`.

### T-NATIVE-RE-1 -- native RE has exactly the critical-line zero locus [Proved]

The executable derivation entry is:

```ns
let centered_re_pattern = () =>
centered_re_perspective()
dual_prime_alignment()
```

`native-space derive --source examples/math-functions.ns centered_re_pattern`
runs the complete primitive trace. That trace proves only source expansion.
The universally quantified monotonicity argument below is the paper proof; the
1.0 runtime does not claim to verify real exponentiation or inequalities.

**Claim.** For symbolic positive input $k$,

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
g(\sigma;k)>0 &\Longleftrightarrow& \sigma<\tfrac{1}{2},\\
g(\sigma;k)=0 &\Longleftrightarrow& \sigma=\tfrac{1}{2},\\
g(\sigma;k)<0 &\Longleftrightarrow& \sigma>\tfrac{1}{2}.
\end{array}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\mathcal P_s\equiv\mathbf0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}.
$$

With the centered input coordinate
$\delta(s)=\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}$, this sign table says

$$
\mathrm{sign}(g(\sigma;k))
=-\mathrm{sign}(\delta(s)),
\qquad
g(\sigma;k)=0\Longleftrightarrow\delta(s)=0.
$$

Thus `centered_re_pattern` and `centered_re_perspective` have the same zero
locus; their off-zero values are not asserted to be equal.

**Proof.** D-PRIME-2 gives $p(k)>1$. By A-LOG-1 the map
$x\mapsto p(k)^{-x}$ is strictly decreasing. If
$\sigma<1-\sigma$, then
$p(k)^{-\sigma}>p(k)^{-(1-\sigma)}$; equality and the reverse inequality
follow in the same way. At equality, injectivity gives
$\sigma=1-\sigma$, hence $2\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=1/2$. The one is the
multiplicative identity used by the native reflection, so the half is forced by
the reflected exponent pair rather than inserted into the zeta value. This
proves all three clauses. Because no step depends on a special value of $k$,
it proves the one symbolic pattern law. A selected observation is already
nonzero off the critical line. $\square$

**Boundary.** This theorem proves the zero locus of the reflected mismatch
pattern. It does not prove that a zeta zero is carried to that pattern zero.

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

Both sides use the same local coefficient convention by

### T-RH-PATTERN-CONSEQUENCE-1 -- RH follows conditionally from the pattern implication [Proved]

**Claim.** T-ZETA-STRIP-1, O-ZETA-RE-CLASSICAL-PATTERN-1, and T-NATIVE-RE-1 imply the
classical Riemann Hypothesis, represented by K-RH-1 in this repository.

**Proof.** Let $s$ be a nontrivial zeta zero in the critical strip.
T-ZETA-STRIP-1 and T-ZETA-OUTPUT-RESIDUAL-1 identify it with an empty
zeta-output residual.
O-ZETA-RE-CLASSICAL-PATTERN-1 makes its native RE pattern law zero.
T-NATIVE-RE-1 then forces
$\mathrm{Re}(s)=\tfrac{1}{2}$, which is K-RH-1. $\square$

**Boundary.** Native RE and the conditional consequence are proved. The
cross-camera zeta-to-RE zero-location implication remains an open RH-equivalent
statement. No `.ns` function is presented as a completed RH proof.
