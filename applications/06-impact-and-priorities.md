# Conditional Impact Ladder and Portfolio Gates

## The possible compounding loop

The large-world impact is conditional on a chain, not implied by the theory:

$$
\text{better representation}
\rightarrow
\text{less memory/bandwidth/compute}
\rightarrow
\text{more affordable experiments and search}
\rightarrow
\text{better models or scientific designs}.
$$

Every arrow can fail. In particular, representation compression may not reduce
wall-clock time; cheaper inference may not improve learning; and larger search
may not produce better scientific outcomes.

## Impact levels

| Level | Evidence required | Legitimate claim |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Definitions and proofs only | Native Space is a coherent formal system |
| 1 | One reproducible equal-budget pilot | Scoped representational gain on named data |
| 2 | Multiple domains/hardware and strong baselines | A reusable compression/dynamics method |
| 3 | End-to-end workflow savings | Lower cost for a named AI or simulation workload |
| 4 | Prospective scientific validation | Faster discovery or better validated predictions |
| 5 | Independent adoption and replication | Broad practical impact |

The project is currently at Level 0.

## What the 1.0 language can establish now

The exact language can prove closed finite residuals equal native zero. The
application witnesses use that ability for distributivity, ORIENT
equivariance, perspective-zero preservation, indexed interaction composition,
and order-independent ADD. Their universal forms are paper-proved consequences
of T-NS-1 and T-PERSPECTIVE-ZERO-1.

Efficiency, predictive quality, physical fidelity, and scientific discovery
are not equalities in the finite algebra. No arrangement of `add`, `multiply`,
`orient`, `index`, and zero equality can establish those claims without a typed
application camera, measured data, and the evaluation protocol. Therefore the
impact ladder remains conditional even though the underlying algebraic
witnesses are exact.

## Portfolio decision gates

### Gate A -- representation

E-MAT-1 must show a Pareto improvement. If it fails across all declared atom
families, pause LLM/JEPA scaling and investigate whether Native Space is only a
descriptive algebra.

### Gate B -- dynamics

E-NBODY-1 must demonstrate rollout and invariant quality, not only trajectory
compression. If it fails, do not claim a generic native dynamical system.

### Gate PL -- programmable self-representation

Typed sequence, record, symbol, and AST cameras must round trip before adding
control flow. Evaluator/compiler agreement must pass before any self-hosting
claim. Exact UTF-8 strings alone do not pass this gate.

### Gate C -- scientific transfer

At least one of E-PROT-1 or E-NS-1 must transfer beyond the tuning regime and
preserve domain observables. If both fail, restrict impact claims to operator
representation.

### Gate D -- systems

E-LLM-1 or E-DYN-1 must produce end-to-end measured savings including
conversion and memory movement. Parameter-count reduction alone does not pass.

## Highest upside by domain

- **AI systems:** lower memory and bandwidth could permit stronger models or
  more inference on fixed hardware, but only after quality and kernel evidence.
- **Molecular science:** cheaper faithful dynamics could enlarge conformational
  search and experimental prioritization, but prospective validation is needed.
- **Engineering and fluids:** compact nonlinear operators could accelerate
  design loops and uncertainty ensembles, but DNS fidelity and conservation
  come first.
- **Celestial dynamics:** compact mode models could accelerate ensembles and
  reveal regime structure, but chaotic predictability limits remain physical.
- **Mathematics:** a new invariant may emerge from successful representations;
  empirical compression alone cannot settle a theorem.
- **Programming systems:** one algebra for data, transformations, and proofs
  could simplify compilers and verification, but only after typed cameras and
  executable semantics are proved.

## Immediate execution order

1. Specify the first matrix atom family and implement E-MAT-1 on synthetic
   controls plus frozen real matrices.
2. Define typed programming-language data cameras and build the calculator
   conformance test.
3. In parallel, define the N-body camera and immutable REBOUND data generator.
4. Select redistribution-compatible MD data and lock E-PROT-1 splits.
5. Pull a bounded JHTDB subset only after the transfer metrics are implemented.
6. Scale into JEPA, diffusion, or an LLM only after a smaller operator gate
   passes.

This ordering maximizes information gained per unit compute and makes a
negative result useful.
